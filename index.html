Key Research Insights

Critical Points & Core Findings

Genetic recombination frequency is not uniformly distributed across physical chromosome lengths during meiotic crossover.

01

Non-Uniform Rate Distribution

Physical length measured in megabases (Mb) does not correspond linearly to genetic distance in centimorgans (cM). Recombination hotspots create distinct variance across intervals.

02

Region A Hyper-Recombination

The "Female CEN to 120" region exhibits a significantly steeper slope (m = 1.40 cM/Mb), reflecting heightened crossover activity compared to the distal tail.

03

Intercept Baseline Shift

The intercept discrepancy (c₁ = -63.69 vs c₂ = -22.64) captures the cumulative baseline offset and physical separation of local chromosomal anchor windows.

Biological Context

Discussion & Conclusions

Recombination Rate Variation Across Meiosis

The fitted line slope directly quantifies the local recombination rate in cM/Mb. The steeper slope in the Female CEN to 120 segment demonstrates that female meiotic crossovers occur with greater probability per physical megabase in this region compared to the terminal 120 to End segment.

Centromeric & Telomeric Structural Anchors

Interpreting the y-intercepts reveals baseline map offsets prior to cumulative recombination accumulation. Discrepancies between physical distance (Mb) and genetic distance (cM) highlight how chromosome architecture influences crossover placement during human female meiosis.
